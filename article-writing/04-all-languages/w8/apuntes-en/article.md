## What Kinds of Apuntes Are on Scribd

The best notes are rarely in the textbooks: they're in the notebooks of whoever already passed the subject. On Scribd they usually show up in four formats:

- **Resúmenes** — the topic condensed into a few pages, made by students.
- **Esquemas and mind maps** — the topic's structure at a glance, ideal for review.
- **Formularios** — formulas and data ready to consult, heavily used in science and engineering.
- **Handwritten scans** — photos or scans of someone's notebook. They come out in the PDF exactly as they look on the page: if the scan is clear, they read fine; if it's blurry, no trick will fix that.

Quick tip for finding yours: search Scribd for the subject name plus your university or your professor. Notes from your own course are worth double, because they follow the same topic order you'll see in class.

An honest limit: only publicly visible content can be downloaded. Private or paid content stays protected.

## Organize Them by Subject — The Folder System

The real problem isn't downloading notes, it's finding them when you need them. Use this structure from day one:

```
Apuntes/
├── Maths/
├── History/
├── Biology/
├── _ToReview/
└── _Archive/
```

**Four rules:**

1. **One folder per subject, nothing else.** Not by term, not by professor: when you review, you think in subjects.
2. **Name files subject-topic-source.** Example: `maths-derivatives-summary.pdf`. Rename at download time — "I'll do it later" never happens.
3. **Use `_ToReview` as your inbox.** Everything new lands there. When you've read and marked it, move it to its subject. Whatever sits there a month unopened you probably didn't need.
4. **Send old stuff to `_Archive`.** Notes from passed subjects don't get deleted (you never know), but they shouldn't get in the way either: archive them.

## Check the PDF Is Complete

Three checks after downloading:

- [ ] **Count the pages.** Compare them with what Scribd shows on the original document. If some are missing, probably only the preview was saved.
- [ ] **Look at the last page.** Endings — conclusions, formula sheets, appendices — are what goes missing most often when an upload is incomplete.
- [ ] **Test readability.** Zoom into a handwritten scan: if you can't read it comfortably on your phone, that PDF won't serve you for review.

## Tips for Printing

- **Print only what you'll review.** Everything else lives happily on your phone. Printing 200 pages "just in case" is wasted paper and money.
- **Two pages per sheet** for long summaries: they read fine and take up half the space.
- **Leave wide margins** for adding your own handwritten notes — notes improve when you complete them yourself.

## Use Them Well — They're Someone's Work

Those notes were made by a fellow student to help, and many carry their copyright. Use them for your personal study: don't sell them, don't publish them as yours, and don't re-upload them elsewhere under your name. If some notes saved you in an exam, a thank-you to the author never hurts. The [fair-use guide](/fair-use) explains it in plain language.

## FAQ

**Do I need an account to download apuntes from Scribd?**
No. Copy the notes' full link, paste it into the downloader, and save the PDF without registering.

**Can I download handwritten scans?**
Yes, as long as they're publicly visible. They'll appear in the PDF exactly as they look on the page.

**What if the notes are incomplete?**
Check the PDF's page count against the original. Sometimes the author uploaded only part of it.

**Can I share the PDF with my classmates?**
Studying among friends is fine, but don't publish or sell it: respect the original author.

**Do notes replace the book or the class?**
No. They're a support for reviewing; the full explanation is in your class and your bibliography.
