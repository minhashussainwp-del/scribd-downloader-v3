## What Is a Makalah?

The makalah is the most common writing task on Indonesian campuses: the lecturer assigns a topic, the student writes a discussion of a dozen pages, then submits it or presents it. A standard makalah's anatomy:

- **Background** — why this topic matters
- **Research questions** — the questions the paper answers
- **Discussion** — the main body: arguments, data, quotes from sources
- **Conclusion** — answers to the research questions
- **Reference list** — the sources used

Because the structure is simple, a makalah is great training material: reading a good one teaches you how to build an argument before you face a thesis.

## Makalah vs Skripsi — Don't Mix Them Up

Many students blur the two. The difference is clear:

| | Makalah | Skripsi |
|---|---|---|
| Nature | Course assignment | Graduation thesis |
| Length | A dozen–dozens of pages | Usually 60–150 pages |
| Research | Primary data not required | Required: method, data, analysis |
| Examined | Graded by the course lecturer | Defended in a thesis examination |
| You need this when | Doing class tasks/presentations, looking for structure examples | Writing your final thesis, needing deep references |

Grabbing the wrong reference is a real problem: citing a coursework paper in your skripsi won't be accepted by your supervisor. If what you're doing is a final thesis, the thesis guide is the right page.

## What to Do with a Downloaded Makalah?

- **Task reference.** Need material for your own paper or presentation? A makalah on the same topic gives you angles and a starter reference list.
- **Structural model.** Reading a tidy makalah shows you how to open a background section, build an argument paragraph by paragraph, and close with a conclusion — skills you use right away.
- **Group discussion material.** One makalah read together beats five people browsing aimlessly.
- **Offline reading.** A PDF reads well anywhere and is easy to cite: just copy its reference list entries to round out your own references.

## Using Someone Else's Makalah the Right Way

This is the part people violate most. The checklist:

- [ ] **Read to understand, not to copy.** The goal is insight and finding sources, not filling your task's pages.
- [ ] **Cite if you take its ideas or sentences.** Name the author and year, like citing any source.
- [ ] **Don't resubmit it as your own.** Swapping the name on the cover of someone else's makalah is plagiarism — and lecturers spot it easily.
- [ ] **Check its reference list, not just its content.** The real treasure is often in the sources its author used.
- [ ] **Rewrite in your own words.** If an argument is good, understand it and explain it again — that's real learning.

## Anatomy of a Good Makalah

Before you leave this page, notice the pattern of makalah that score well — use it as a model for your task:

- Its background is specific, not generic sentences that fit any topic.
- Each discussion paragraph carries one main idea supported by sources.
- Its conclusion answers the research questions instead of repeating the discussion.
- Its reference list contains sources actually cited in the text, not filler.

## An Honest Word on Plagiarism

Downloading a makalah to study its structure and use it as reference is perfectly fine. Copying its content — let alone submitting it under your name — is plagiarism, and campuses check assignments with detection software. Read, learn the pattern, write your own version. That simple.
