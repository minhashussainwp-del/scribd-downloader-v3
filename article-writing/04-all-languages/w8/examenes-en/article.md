## The Mock-Exam Routine — Practice with a Timer

Re-reading notes gives a false sense of knowing. Solving exams under time pressure doesn't. This 4-step routine turns every PDF into a real mock exam:

1. **Print or prepare the exam.** On paper or in an annotation app — but with the answer key out of sight.
2. **Start the timer.** Use the real exam's time if you know it; if not, take about 2 minutes per question as a starting point and adjust as you practice.
3. **Solve without breaks.** No phone, no peeking at notes. If you get stuck, move on and come back at the end — exactly like exam day.
4. **Correct the same day.** With the answer key, mark every mistake and log it (next section). Correcting a week later doesn't work: the mistake has gone cold.

Repeat with another exam every few days. Improvement comes from repetition, not from stacking unopened PDFs.

One detail that makes the difference: alternate subjects in your mocks. If you only practice your strong subject, the error log stays empty and the confidence is fake. The topics you struggle with most deserve the most mocks.

## Your Error Log

Every mistake you make in a mock goes into this table — in a notebook or a simple sheet:

| Date | Exam | Question/topic | My mistake | What I'll review |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 12/09 | Algebra 2023 | Equations | Signs | Review sign rules |
| 14/09 | Algebra 2022 | Functions | Graphs | Draw 10 graphs |

**Why it works:** after three mocks you'll see your weak topics repeat. Those — not the whole syllabus — are what you should review. Without a log, you repeat what you already know and avoid what you don't.

## Watch Out for Shared Answer Keys

Answer keys uploaded by other students are a huge help, but nobody checks them before publishing. Before memorizing an answer:

- **Cross-check with your notes or book** when a solution looks odd.
- **Distrust shortcuts with no explanation** — a result with no working teaches you nothing.
- **If you spot a clear error**, note it on your PDF so you don't trip twice.

## Tips for Printing

- **Print the exam and the answer key separately.** If they're together, the temptation to peek at the answers is too strong.
- **Single-sided** for exams you'll solve by hand — you need the space.
- **Keep solved exams** in a folder: they're your progress history.

## Use Them Well

Exam material is shared by students and teachers to help. Use it for your personal preparation: don't sell it or publish it as your own. And remember that practicing with past exams is a complement — the foundation is still understanding the topics. The [fair-use guide](/fair-use) explains it in plain language.

## FAQ

**Do I need an account to download exámenes from Scribd?**
No. Copy the full link, paste it into the downloader, and save the PDF without registering.

**Do exams come with solutions?**
Some do, some don't. Check the description and the document's pages on Scribd before downloading.

**What do I do if the PDF comes out incomplete?**
Compare the pages with the original. If only the preview was saved, look for another upload of the same exam.

**How often should I do a mock?**
It depends on your calendar, but consistency beats quantity: one well-corrected mock every few days beats five uncorrected ones.

**Can I share the exams with my study group?**
Preparing together is fine, but don't publish or sell them: respect whoever shared them.
