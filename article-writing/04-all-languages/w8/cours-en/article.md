# Download Cours Course Notes on Scribd for Free and Revise Well

To download a cours (French-style course material) from Scribd in PDF for free, copy the full link of the course's page, paste it into this site's free downloader, and get your PDF. No account, nothing to install: whatever is publicly visible on the page is what you get in the file.

**Key takeaways**

- Copy the link → paste it → download the PDF.
- Works for the professor's slides, your class's fiches de révision (revision sheets), and polycopiés (course handouts) — as long as they're public.
- Downloading is only half the job: below is a method for turning your cours into revision cards.
- These documents are someone's work: keep them for revising, don't resell them.

## What Kinds of Cours Are on Scribd

Three formats show up often:

- **Lecture slides (diapositives)** — the professor's slides, shared by students. Each slide becomes one page of the PDF: they read very well offline.
- **Fiches de révision** — the course condensed by someone in your class, often clearer than the original handout.
- **Polycopiés** — long, complete documents, handy to keep as PDFs for finding a chapter in two seconds thanks to text search.

An honest limit: only publicly visible documents download. Private or paid content stays protected — and that's fair.

## How to Download — 3 Steps

1. **Copy the link.** Open the cours on Scribd and copy the full URL from the address bar — the document page, not the search page.
2. **Paste it into the downloader.** Paste the link into the box at the top of this page and start the process.
3. **Save and rename the PDF.** Your file is ready to download — rename it right away: "document.pdf" will tell you nothing in a month.

First time? The [step-by-step guide](/how-it-works) details each part.

## Organize Your Cours by Subject

The real problem isn't downloading, it's finding things again. Adopt this structure from the start:

```
Cours/
├── Maths/
├── History/
├── Biology/
├── _ToReread/
└── _Archive/
```

**Four rules:**

1. **One folder per subject.** When you revise, you think in subjects, not terms.
2. **Name files subject-chapter-source.** Example: `maths-derivatives-fiche.pdf`. Rename at download time — "I'll do it later" never happens.
3. **Use `_ToReread` as your inbox.** Everything new lands there. When you've read and annotated it, move it to its subject. Whatever has slept there for a month you probably didn't need.
4. **Archive the old.** Cours from subjects you've already passed don't get deleted (you never know), but they shouldn't get in the way: off to `_Archive`.

## The "Cours → Fiche" Method in 3 Steps

A downloaded cours that you only re-read fades fast. Turn it into a personal fiche:

1. **Read the PDF through once**, highlighting definitions, dates, and formulas.
2. **Rewrite the essentials on a fiche** — one page per chapter, in your own words. If you can't explain it simply, you haven't understood it: go back to the cours.
3. **Re-read the fiche, not the cours.** As the exam approaches, only the fiche counts. The PDF stays in reserve to check a detail.

A fiche you wrote yourself is worth ten downloaded cours never opened.

## Check the PDF Is Complete

Three checks after every download:

- [ ] **Count the pages.** Compare with the number shown on Scribd. If some are missing, only the preview was saved.
- [ ] **Look at the last page.** Conclusions and appendices sit at the end — and that's what most often goes missing when an upload is incomplete.
- [ ] **Test readability.** Zoom into a page: if you can't read it comfortably on your phone, that PDF won't serve you for revision.

## Printing Tips

- **Print slides 2 or 4 per page**: they read very well and divide the paper by two or four.
- **Print only what you'll revise.** The rest lives happily on your phone.
- **Leave wide margins** to add your own handwritten notes — a cours improves when you complete it.

## Respect the Authors' Work

Cours and fiches shared on Scribd are someone's work — sometimes a classmate, sometimes a teacher. Keep them for your personal use: don't resell them and don't republish them under your name. If a fiche saved you, remember to thank its author. The [fair-use guide](/fair-use) explains it all in plain language.

## FAQ

**Do I need an account to download a cours from Scribd as PDF?**
No. Copy the course's full link, paste it into the downloader, and get your PDF without registering.

**Do slides come through well in PDF?**
Yes. Each slide becomes one page of the PDF, which reads very well offline.

**What if the cours is incomplete?**
Compare the PDF's page count with the original on Scribd. The author sometimes only uploaded part of it.

**Can I share the PDF with my class?**
For revising among friends, no problem — but don't publish it elsewhere and don't sell it: respect the original author.

**Is a downloaded cours enough to pass?**
No. It's a revision aid; understanding comes from the course itself and your own work.

## To Finish

A good cours on your phone, organized by subject and turned into fiches, changes a revision session. Download, organize, fiche — in that order. The box at the top of the page is your first step.
