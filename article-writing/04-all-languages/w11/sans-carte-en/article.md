## Why Do People Search for "Without a Credit Card"?

Because Scribd runs on subscriptions, and a subscription requires card details. But most people only want one or two documents: a study guide before an exam, a manual for work, sheet music for the weekend. Not a monthly charge.

Others simply don't have a credit card, or refuse to hand one to a website they just found. They're right to be careful: signing up for a "free trial" with your card often ends in a forgotten subscription that renews itself month after month. When you need one file, the simplest option is a tool that asks for nothing at all.

Keep that caution when you look at downloader sites too: if a tool calls itself free but wants your card, something is wrong.

## Do You Actually Need a Subscription?

Answer these four questions honestly:

- [ ] **You read on Scribd several times a week.** A subscription may be worth it: full access and offline reading in their app.
- [ ] **You need one or two specific documents.** The free tool is enough. A monthly plan for a single file makes no sense.
- [ ] **You just want to try before deciding.** Start with the free tool, decide later — without giving your card to anyone.
- [ ] **The document is subscriber-only** (locked preview, hidden pages). No free tool will open it: only a subscription can.

If you checked the first box, get the subscription with your eyes open — and set a calendar reminder to cancel. In every other case, don't give your card to anyone.

## The Trap of "Free" Sites That Ask for Your Card

Some sites promise a free download, then demand your card details "for verification," "to confirm your identity," or "to activate your free account." That is the clearest warning sign there is: a genuinely free tool has no reason to know your card.

Other warning signs:

- **The trial that converts itself** into a paid plan after a few days, with cancellation buried deep in a menu.
- **The "free" download** that first requires email sign-up, then a card "to open the download."
- **Vague fees** in endless terms and conditions, often in legal jargon.

The rule is simple and has no exceptions: if a free tool asks for your credit card, close the tab.

The tool handles documents that are publicly visible on Scribd. Private content and subscriber-only material stay protected: no honest free tool can open them, and distrust any site that claims it can.

The file you get is a clean PDF, with no watermark or added marks. Temporary working files are deleted automatically after processing, and your downloads are never published or shared.
