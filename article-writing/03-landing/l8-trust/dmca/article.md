# DMCA Copyright Compliance & Takedown Policy

This page explains how this service handles copyright under the DMCA (Digital Millennium Copyright Act). If you're a copyright holder and your work is being processed through this tool without your permission, here's what a takedown notice is, what a valid one contains, and where to send it.

**Key takeaways**

- We comply with the DMCA and act on valid takedown notices.
- A valid notice needs six specific elements — listed below as a checklist.
- Notices go through the site's contact page (copyright topic).
- If a document was excluded by mistake, the affected party can file a counter-notice.
- This page is general information, not legal advice.

## Our Compliance Stance

We respect the intellectual property rights of authors, publishers, and creators. When we receive a valid DMCA takedown notice, we review it and act on it. This tool exists for people to keep personal copies of documents they have the right to keep — not to redistribute other people's copyrighted work.

## How the Service Handles Files

Some context on what "removal" means here. This tool isn't a document library — it doesn't host a public collection of files or republish anyone's work. When you paste a link, the document is processed on the server to generate your PDF, temporary working files are cleaned up automatically afterward, and a short-term cached copy may be kept so repeat requests load faster. Nothing is published or shared publicly.

So a takedown here means excluding a document from being processed — the file can't be pulled through the tool anymore. It doesn't affect the original document on Scribd itself; that's Scribd's own process to handle.

## What a DMCA Notice Is

A DMCA takedown notice is a formal request from a copyright holder (or someone authorized to act for one) asking a service to stop providing access to material they believe infringes their copyright. It's a standard process used across the web — the same basic format works whether you're writing to a hosting company, a search engine, or a tool like this one.

Filing one isn't a lawsuit. It's a notice: "this is my work, it's being used without my permission, please stop." Services that comply with the DMCA take valid notices seriously because the law protects services that do — and doesn't protect those that ignore them.

## Filing a Notice: The Six Required Elements

For a notice to be valid, it needs all six of these. Use this as a checklist before you send:

1. **Identify the copyrighted work.** Describe your work — title, and a link to the original if it's published online.
2. **Identify the infringing material.** The specific Scribd URL being processed through this tool. A general complaint without a specific link can't be acted on.
3. **Your contact information.** Full name, organization (if any), address, phone number, and email — somewhere we can reach you about the notice.
4. **A good-faith statement.** A statement that you believe, in good faith, that the use of the material isn't authorized by the copyright owner, its agent, or the law.
5. **An accuracy statement.** A statement, made under penalty of perjury, that the information in your notice is accurate and that you're authorized to act on behalf of the copyright owner.
6. **Your signature.** Physical or electronic — a typed full name counts as an electronic signature.

Missing pieces slow everything down. The most common reason a notice can't be processed is element 2: no specific link. Include it.

## Where to Send It

Use the site's contact page and choose the copyright topic, including all six items above. That's the channel that's monitored for these requests — sending it anywhere else risks it sitting unread.

## What Happens After You File

Here's the process in plain language:

1. **We receive your notice** through the contact page.
2. **We check it's complete** — all six elements present, specific link included.
3. **We act on valid notices** — the document is excluded from processing so it can't be pulled through the tool anymore.
4. **We keep a record** of the notice and the action taken.

How fast that happens depends on volume and complexity — there's no fixed legal clock we can promise here. What we can say: complete notices with a specific link are the ones that move fastest, because there's nothing to go back and ask about.

## Counter-Notices

Sometimes exclusions are mistakes — a document gets flagged that shouldn't have been. If you believe a document was excluded wrongly, you can send a counter-notice with:

- Your contact details
- Identification of the excluded material (the specific link)
- A statement that you consent to the jurisdiction of your local courts
- Your signature (physical or electronic)

One honest recommendation: get legal advice before filing a counter-notice. The statements involved carry real weight, and a lawyer can tell you in ten minutes whether your situation qualifies.

## FAQ

**What is a DMCA takedown notice?**
A formal request from a copyright holder asking a service to stop providing access to material they believe infringes their copyright. It needs six specific elements to be valid — listed in the checklist above.

**How do I report my copyrighted work here?**
Send a notice through the site's contact page (copyright topic) with all six required elements, including the specific Scribd URL.

**Do I need a lawyer to file a notice?**
No — any copyright holder or their authorized agent can file. But if your situation is complicated, legal advice helps.

**What if I filed a notice and nothing happened?**
First check: did your notice include all six elements, especially the specific link? Incomplete notices can't be processed. If it was complete, follow up through the same contact channel.

**Can a blocked document be restored?**
Yes, through the counter-notice process described above — if the exclusion was a mistake.

**Does a takedown here remove the document from Scribd?**
No. It only stops the document from being processed through this tool. The original on Scribd is Scribd's to handle.

## The Bottom Line

Copyright holders have a clear path: a valid six-element notice through the contact page, and we act on it. Users have a clear boundary: this tool is for documents you have the right to keep. The fair-use page explains that side of the line in plain language.
